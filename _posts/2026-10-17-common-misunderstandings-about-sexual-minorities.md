### A myth-busting reference, with what the evidence actually says

*Scope: lesbian, gay, bisexual, asexual and other non-heterosexual people, plus trans and gender-diverse
people where the confusion overlaps (in Chinese usage, 性少数 / LGBT usually covers both). Focus: claims
that circulate in Chinese families, schools and media, with the international evidence that answers them.*

> **How to use this.** Each entry gives the myth (often with the common Chinese phrasing), what is
> actually true, and why the myth persists. Not every "myth" is a lie — some are half-truths, and two
> entries below are flagged as **contested** because the honest answer is more complicated than either
> side claims.

---

## 0. Five terms that prevent half the confusion

| Term | What it means | What it is *not* |
|---|---|---|
| **Sex assigned at birth** (生理性别) | The biological classification (male/female/intersex) recorded at birth | Not the same as gender identity |
| **Gender identity** (性别认同) | A person's internal sense of being a man, a woman, both, or neither | Not a sexual preference |
| **Sexual orientation** (性取向) | Who someone is attracted to (same-sex, other-sex, both, neither) | Not a choice of "lifestyle" |
| **Gender expression** (性别表达) | How someone dresses, speaks, behaves | Not proof of orientation or identity |
| **Trans / cis** (跨性别 / 顺性别) | Whether one's gender identity matches the sex assigned at birth | Trans is about identity, not orientation |

**The single most common error:** treating *orientation* (who you want) and *identity* (who you are) as
one thing. A trans woman can be straight, lesbian or bisexual; a feminine man is usually not trans.

## 1. Quick-reference table (20 myths)

| # | Myth | Reality |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "LGBT people are a tiny, recent phenomenon." | Same-sex behaviour is documented across every era and culture; what changed is visibility and labelling. |
| 2 | "Homosexuality is a Western import to China." | Chinese history records 断袖, 龙阳, 男风, 磨镜 and same-sex unions in Fujian; the *category* is modern, the *people* are not. |
| 3 | "It's a choice / a lifestyle." | No major scientific body treats orientation as voluntarily chosen; it is not a decision, though behaviour is. |
| 4 | "It's caused by bad parenting." | No evidence for the "absent father / smothering mother" theory; it is a mid-20th-century psychoanalytic conjecture. |
| 5 | "It's caused by abuse or being seduced." | Abuse is not a cause of orientation; this myth blames victims and fuels stigma. |
| 6 | "It can be cured / converted." | Conversion practices are rejected by WHO, APA and most professional bodies, and are associated with harm. |
| 7 | "It's a mental illness." | Removed from the DSM in 1973 and from the ICD in 1990; ICD-11 (2019) moved gender variance out of mental disorders entirely. |
| 8 | "Gay men are paedophiles." | A smear with no basis; child sexual abuse is overwhelmingly committed by people who identify as heterosexual. |
| 9 | "All gay men are feminine, all lesbians masculine." | Gender expression varies; conflating it with orientation erases masculine gay men and feminine lesbians. |
| 10 | "Bisexuals are confused / greedy / just experimenting." | Bisexuality is a stable orientation; "confusion" is a stereotype, not a clinical finding. |
| 11 | "Trans women are just gay men who went too far." | Gender identity ≠ orientation; this conflates two separate dimensions. |
| 12 | "Being trans means wanting surgery." | Many trans people never have surgery; transition is social, legal, medical or any combination. |
| 13 | "Trans people are a 'third gender'." | Some cultures have third-gender roles, but most trans people identify as men or women. |
| 14 | "Detransition is common — people regret it." | Regret rates in follow-up studies are low (roughly 1–2% in most series); most detransition is driven by family, money or discrimination, not by change of identity. |
| 15 | "Same-sex couples can't raise healthy children." | Decades of research find no meaningful difference in child outcomes; the variable that matters is family stress, not family structure. |
| 16 | "LGBT people are promiscuous / can't hold relationships." | Relationship stability tracks social support and legal recognition, not orientation. |
| 17 | "Asexual people are sick, or just haven't met the right person." | Asexuality is a sexual orientation (little or no sexual attraction), not a disorder or a delay. |
| 18 | "Everyone should come out — it's liberation." | Coming out is a personal risk calculation; for dependants, being outed can mean losing housing or funding. |
| 19 | "China has no law about this, so nothing is regulated." | Homosexuality was removed from the criminal "hooliganism" offence in 1997 and from the diagnostic manual in 2001, but there is still no anti-discrimination law and no same-sex marriage. |
| 20 | "There's no discrimination any more — it's all resolved." | Legal equality and social acceptance are uneven and, in several countries, currently regressing. |

## 2. About the *causes* of orientation

**Myth: "It's a choice."**
Orientation is not a decision; behaviour is. No major psychological or medical body regards same-sex
orientation as voluntarily selected, and attempts to change it have not been shown to work.

**Myth: "It's the parents' fault."**
The "cold, distant father and overbearing mother" theory came from mid-20th-century psychoanalysis and
has failed replication for decades. It survives because it offers parents a target for guilt and blame.

**Myth: "Something happened to them."**
Sexual abuse, seduction, pornography and "too much internet" are routinely cited as causes. Abuse
survivors are frequently told their orientation is damage — a claim that both misrepresents the
evidence and compounds trauma.

**Honest caveat:** science describes orientation as arising from a combination of genetic, hormonal and
early developmental factors, with no single cause identified, and it is not fully understood. "We don't
know the exact mechanism" is the accurate answer — and it cuts against the "choice" story, because a
trait that is not chosen cannot be a moral failing.

## 3. About "cure"

**Myth: "It can be cured."**
There is no reliable evidence that any intervention changes orientation. Major professional bodies —
the WHO, the American Psychological Association, the American Psychiatric Association and others —
oppose conversion practices, and research links them to depression, anxiety and suicidality.

**Chinese legal reality:** courts have repeatedly sided against providers. In the 2014 Haidian case
(彭某诉重庆心语飘香心理咨询中心人格权案) a clinic advertising "homosexual conversion" was found liable,
and in 2017 a Henan court ruled against a psychiatric hospital that had detained a man for "treatment"
after his family discovered he was gay ([report](https://news.ifeng.com/a/20170703/51364696_0.shtml)).
Practices persist mainly in unregulated counselling and "family therapy" settings.

**Why it persists:** families want a fix; the market supplies one; and the language of "treatment"
borrows medical authority.

## 4. About identity and body

**Myth: "Trans people are really just very gay."** Orientation and identity are different axes.
A trans woman attracted to women is a lesbian; a trans man attracted to men is a gay man. Neither is
"a gay person who transitioned".

**Myth: "Trans = surgery."** Genital surgery is one option among many, and a minority path globally.
Social transition, hormonal therapy, legal recognition, or none of the above are all real.

**Myth: "Desistance is the norm for children."** Some children's gender expression changes; others'
identities persist. Blanket claims in either direction outrun the evidence. Clinical guidance
emphasises supportive, non-coercive exploration rather than either affirmation-by-default or
conversion-style pressure.

**Myth: "Detransition proves transition was a mistake."** Detransition is uncommon and often driven by
cost, family rejection or discrimination. Regret rates in long-term follow-up are low. The existence of
a few regretful people does not generalise to a population.

## 5. About family, children and "social harm"

**Myth: "Children raised by same-sex parents are harmed."**
Systematic reviews and professional bodies (including the APA and the American Academy of Pediatrics)
find no meaningful differences in psychological adjustment, school outcomes or attachment. Where
differences appear, they track family stress and stigma.

**Myth: "Acceptance will make society collapse / the birth rate fall."**
This is a values claim dressed as an empirical one. Fertility is driven by economics, housing, childcare
and policy — not by the legal status of a minority.

**Myth: "It's a rich-country issue."**
Sexual and gender minorities exist everywhere; what differs is whether they can be visible. Framing it
as "Western" is a political move that erases local history and local people.

## 6. About law and policy — two **contested** items

**"China removed homosexuality from its mental disorder list in 2001."**
This is the standard account: the Chinese Classification of Mental Disorders (CCMD-3, 2001) dropped
homosexuality as a diagnostic category, following the DSM (1973) and ICD (1990). **Recent scholarship
questions whether that amounted to formal depathologisation**, since the CCMD is produced by a
professional society and no equivalent health-ministry declaration followed — see, for example, the
University of Hong Kong law faculty discussion
["A Lost Story: When Did China Depathologize Homosexuality?"](https://www.law.hku.hk/events/ccl-talk-20250602/).
The practical point stands — it is no longer a routine psychiatric diagnosis in China — but the
bureaucratic details are murkier than the slogan suggests.

**"China has no laws concerning homosexuality."**
Also too simple. The 1997 Criminal Law dropped the "hooliganism" (流氓罪) offence that had been used to
prosecute same-sex conduct; there is no sodomy law. But there is **no anti-discrimination law covering
sexual orientation or gender identity**, no same-sex marriage or partnership, and no pathway to legal
gender recognition that does not require surgery. Same-sex couples sometimes use adult guardianship
(意定监护) as a partial legal workaround for medical and property decisions.

**Why this matters for students:** the absence of protection is not the absence of regulation. Rules
about "public order and good morals" (公序良俗) in campus conduct codes can still be applied
discretionarily.

## 7. About social life and coming out

**Myth: "Coming out is always the right thing to do."**
Coming out is associated with better wellbeing *when the environment is safe*. For a student financially
dependent on hostile parents, it can mean losing housing, tuition or contact. The ethical rule is
autonomy: the person decides, on their own timeline.

**Myth: "If you don't disclose, you're dishonest."**
Privacy is not deception. Not volunteering information about your sexuality is normal boundary-setting
that heterosexual people do constantly.

**Myth: "Bisexuals have it easy — they can just pass as straight."**
Passing is not acceptance; bi people report elevated rates of invisibility, dismissal from both
heterosexual and gay communities, and worse mental-health outcomes than gay or lesbian peers in some
surveys.

## 8. Why these myths survive

1. **Pathologisation left a vocabulary.** Terms like 易性癖 and "性身份异常" are still in circulation —
   and still in some university curricula.
2. **Visibility is recent and uneven.** Most families have no openly LGBT relative, so media stereotypes
   fill the gap.
3. **AIDS stigma.** The 1980s framing of HIV as a "gay disease" still contaminates public discussion.
4. **Confirmation loops.** A family that believes "it's a phase" reads every fluid identity as proof,
   and never sees the stable majority.
5. **Censorship and self-censorship.** Where content is filtered, accurate information circulates less
   than rumour.
6. **Institutional incentives.** Conversion-therapy providers, "family harmony" counsellors and
   clickbait media all profit from the myths.

## 9. How to correct a myth without starting a fight

- **Answer the question behind the question.** "Is it curable?" usually means "Will my child be okay?"
- **Use one fact, not ten.** A single, checkable claim ("the WHO removed it from the disease
  classification in 1990") beats a lecture.
- **Separate facts from values.** "Research finds no difference in child outcomes" is a fact; "and
  therefore it's fine" is a value. Say which one you're asserting.
- **Don't debate your own existence.** You are not obliged to litigate your identity with someone
  arguing in bad faith. Offer a source and leave.
- **Name the cost.** Conversion practices are linked to depression and suicide risk — that is often the
  most persuasive fact for a worried parent.
- **Know what's contested.** Conceding "the exact causes aren't fully understood" buys credibility; it
  does not concede the moral point.
